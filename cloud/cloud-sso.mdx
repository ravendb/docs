# Cloud: External SSO & User Management
<Admonition type="note" title="">

**External SSO & User Management** lets your team sign in to RavenDB Cloud through your organization's own
identity provider (IdP) — Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Google, or any other OpenID Connect (OIDC) provider.
Optionally, the identity provider can also create, update and deactivate those users automatically
through **SCIM 2.0**, and map its groups to RavenDB Cloud roles.

Authentication stays with your identity provider. Authorization stays in RavenDB Cloud: what a user may
do is still decided by [account roles](../cloud/cloud-account.mdx#account-roles) and
[product permissions](../cloud/cloud-account.mdx#product-permissions).

## Availability

The feature is listed under **Account features** in the Portal [sidebar](../cloud/portal/cloud-portal.mdx#sidebar-views),
on the **External SSO & User Management** card.

External SSO & User Management has to be activated for your account by RavenDB Cloud before you can
configure it. Until then, the card shows a **Not available** badge and a **Contact sales** button that
opens an email to our sales team.

Once the feature is activated:

* **Not configured** — click **Configure** to open the [connect wizard](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#connect-your-identity-provider).
* **Configured** — click **Manage** to open the SSO settings page.

The card's **See documentation** link opens this page.

## Who can configure SSO

| **Role** | **What it allows** |
|----------|--------------------|
| **External SSO & User Management** | Everything on this page: create and edit the connection, generate and revoke SCIM tokens, change the management type, reset identity links, and disable SSO. Account owners hold this role. |
| **Account with User Management** | Once a connection exists: open the SSO settings and edit the [group to role mapping](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#group-to-role-mapping). In *Sign-in only* mode, add SSO users on the [Users & Access](../cloud/portal/cloud-portal-users-and-access-tab.mdx) page. It cannot change the connection, the SCIM tokens or the management type. |

A member without the **External SSO & User Management** role who opens a card that is not configured
yet sees *An account admin needs to set this up first.*

See [Account roles](../cloud/cloud-account.mdx#account-roles) for the full list of roles.

## Management types

You choose one of two management types when you connect your identity provider.

| | **Sign-in only** | **Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)** |
|---|---|---|
| Who signs users in | Your identity provider | Your identity provider |
| Who creates SSO users | You, on the [Users & Access](../cloud/portal/cloud-portal-users-and-access-tab.mdx) page (**Add new SSO user**) | Your identity provider, through SCIM |
| Who removes SSO users | You, on the Users & Access page | Your identity provider (deactivating or unassigning a user) |
| Where SSO users' roles are set | Users & Access | Your identity provider's groups, through the [group to role mapping](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#group-to-role-mapping) |
| Required at the identity provider | OpenID Connect | OpenID Connect and outbound SCIM 2.0 |

<Admonition type="note" title="SSO users and Cloud users">
An **SSO user** signs in only through your identity provider. A **Cloud user** signs in with a magic link
or with Google, GitHub or Microsoft, as described in [Login](../cloud/cloud-overview.mdx#login-to-your-account).

Turning SSO on does not remove Cloud users: existing members keep their access, and you can still invite
new Cloud users in either management type. Deprovisioning someone at your identity provider removes only
their SSO user, not a Cloud user the same person may also have. If you want your identity provider to
be the only way into the account, remove the other Cloud members yourself.
</Admonition>

<Admonition type="warning" title="Changing the management type later">
You can switch between the two types later from the SSO settings page (**Change management type**).
The switch asks you to type your account domain to confirm, because it **permanently removes every
SSO user** on the connection. Leaving *Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)* also deletes the SCIM
tokens, the discovered groups and the group mapping. When you switch to SCIM, your identity provider
re-creates the users it manages on its next push.
</Admonition>

## Identity provider requirements

Any identity provider that implements the standard OpenID Connect authorization code flow works, as
long as it meets these requirements:

| **Requirement** | **Details** |
|-----------------|-------------|
| Discovery document | The issuer must serve `/.well-known/openid-configuration`. All endpoints are read from it; they cannot be entered by hand. |
| HTTPS issuer | The issuer must use HTTPS and a public host name — not an IP address, and no user name or password in the URL. Redirects from the discovery or token endpoint are not followed. |
| Client authentication | The client ID and secret are sent in the request body (`client_secret_post`). A provider that accepts only HTTP Basic client authentication (`client_secret_basic`) fails at the token exchange. |
| ID token claims | `sub` and `email` must both be present in the **ID token** itself. RavenDB Cloud does not call the UserInfo endpoint. |
| Verified email | If the ID token marks the email as unverified (`email_verified: false`), sign-in is refused. A token without the claim is accepted. |
| Scopes | `openid profile email`. The scopes are fixed; allow them for the application at your identity provider. |
| Redirect URL | `https://cloud.ravendb.net/api/v1/sso/callback`, registered at your identity provider exactly as shown — no trailing slash or extra characters. |
| SCIM (optional) | For *Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)*: an identity provider that pushes users and groups to a SCIM 2.0 endpoint with a bearer token, such as Okta or Microsoft Entra ID (P1 or P2). |

<Admonition type="note" title="">
A `groups` claim in the ID token is ignored. Roles come only from SCIM group membership and your
[group to role mapping](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#group-to-role-mapping).
</Admonition>

## Connect your identity provider

Before you start, create an OpenID Connect application at your identity provider - see
[Provider setup guides](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#provider-setup-guides). You need its **issuer**,
**client ID** and **client secret**.

1. In the Portal sidebar, open **Account features** and click **Configure** on the
   **External SSO & User Management** card.
2. **Management type** — choose **Sign-in only** or **Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)**.
   See [Management types](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#management-types).
3. **Identity provider** — enter your OpenID Connect details:
   * **Issuer / discovery URL** — the issuer of your identity provider, e.g. `https://idp.example.com`.
   * **Client ID** and **Client secret** — from the application you created.
   * **Scopes** — shown for reference: `openid profile email`.
4. **Redirect URL** — copy the URL shown and add it to the application's redirect (callback) URLs at
   your identity provider. It must match exactly.
5. **Review** — check the details and click **Create connection**.

The connection is created and the SSO settings page opens. A **Finish setting up SSO** banner lists the
remaining steps.

<Admonition type="warning" title="Copy the SCIM token now">
With **Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)**, creating the connection also creates the first SCIM token
and shows it together with the **SCIM base URL**. The token is displayed only once — copy both values
and enter them in your identity provider's provisioning settings.
See [SCIM provisioning](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#scim-provisioning).
</Admonition>

## Provider setup guides

Every provider needs the same three things: an OpenID Connect **web application** that uses the
authorization code flow, the [Redirect URL](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#identity-provider-requirements) registered
on it, and its **issuer**, **client ID** and **client secret** entered in the
[connect wizard](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#connect-your-identity-provider). Assign the application to the people who
should sign in.

### Okta

* Create an app integration of type **OIDC - OpenID Connect**, application type **Web Application**.
* Add the Redirect URL as a **Sign-in redirect URI**, and use **Client secret** for client authentication.
* Issuer: your Okta domain, e.g. `https://your-company.okta.com`. If you use a custom authorization
  server, use its issuer instead, e.g. `https://your-company.okta.com/oauth2/default`.
* For *Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)*, configure SCIM provisioning with:
  * **SCIM connector base URL** — the SCIM base URL from the Portal.
  * **Unique identifier field for users** — `userName`.
  * **Authentication Mode** — **HTTP Header**, with the SCIM token as the bearer token.
  * Enable creating, updating and deactivating users, and push the groups you want to
    [map to roles](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#group-to-role-mapping).

### Microsoft Entra ID

* Register an application (**App registrations** > **New registration**) and add the Redirect URL with
  the **Web** platform.
* Create a client secret under **Certificates & secrets**, and copy its **value**.
* Under **Token configuration**, add the optional **email** claim to the **ID** token, so that the ID
  token carries the user's email address.
* Issuer: `https://login.microsoftonline.com/<tenant-id>/v2.0`. The client ID is the
  **Application (client) ID**.
* For *Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)* (requires Microsoft Entra ID P1 or P2): in the application's
  **Enterprise application** > **Provisioning**, set **Provisioning Mode** to **Automatic**, enter the
  SCIM base URL as **Tenant URL** and the SCIM token as **Secret Token**, and test the connection.

Microsoft Entra ID does not send an `email_verified` claim; RavenDB Cloud accepts its tokens without it.

### Google

* In the Google Cloud console, create an **OAuth client ID** of type **Web application**, and add the
  Redirect URL to its **Authorized redirect URIs**.
* Issuer: `https://accounts.google.com`.
* To limit sign-in to your Google Workspace organization, set the OAuth consent screen's user type to
  **Internal**.
* Google Workspace does not provision users to custom SCIM applications, so use **Sign-in only** and add
  your team on the Users & Access page.

### Other OpenID Connect providers

Auth0, Keycloak, Ping Identity, OneLogin and other providers work the same way: create a regular web
application (confidential client) with the authorization code flow, allow the `client_secret_post`
client authentication method, register the Redirect URL, and enter the issuer exactly as the provider's
discovery document states it (for Keycloak: `https://<host>/realms/<realm>`).

Use **Sign-in only** with providers that cannot push users to an external SCIM endpoint, such as Auth0.

## SCIM provisioning

With **Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)**, your identity provider pushes users and groups to
RavenDB Cloud:

* Assigning a user to the application creates an SSO user in your account.
* Profile changes are applied to that SSO user.
* Deactivating or unassigning the user removes them from the account.
* Group memberships drive the user's roles through the [group to role mapping](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#group-to-role-mapping).

SCIM never changes the **account owner**.

Configure your identity provider with:

| **Setting** | **Value** |
|-------------|-----------|
| SCIM base URL | The **SCIM base URL** shown on the SSO settings page. Enter it as shown — your identity provider appends `/Users` and `/Groups` itself. |
| Authentication | HTTP header bearer token: the **SCIM token** issued in the Portal. An API key does not work here. |

**SCIM tokens**

* The connect wizard creates the first token. To create another one, click **Generate SCIM token** on the
  **SCIM provisioning** card of the SSO settings page.
* A token is shown only once, when it is created. Save it somewhere safe.
* Tokens do not expire. Revoke a token you no longer use.
* You can have several active tokens, for example to rotate them without downtime.
* Managing tokens requires the **External SSO & User Management** role.

SCIM requests are accepted only while the connection uses **Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)**.

## Group to role mapping

In **Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)** mode, the groups your identity provider pushes appear under
**Discovered groups** on the SSO settings page. Edit a group's mapping to set:

* **Defined roles** — the [account roles](../cloud/cloud-account.mdx#account-roles) that members of the group receive.
* **Group access** — the members' [product permissions](../cloud/cloud-account.mdx#product-permissions), including
  the default access for new products.

How the mapping is applied:

* A user who belongs to several groups receives the roles and product permissions of all of them combined.
* A change to the mapping applies to the group's members immediately.
* Any account role can be mapped. A member who is not the account owner can map only roles and product
  permissions they hold themselves.
* In this mode, SSO users are locked on the Users & Access page: their roles and product permissions
  change only through the mapping.

<Admonition type="warning" title="">
Anyone who can change group membership in your identity provider can, through the mapping, grant the
roles mapped to that group — including **Account with User Management** and
**External SSO & User Management**. Map those roles only to groups whose membership you control.
</Admonition>

## Signing in with SSO

SSO users sign in from the RavenDB Cloud [login page](https://cloud.ravendb.net/user/login):

1. Click **Sign in with External SSO**.
2. Enter your **Account domain** and click **Continue with SSO**.
3. Sign in at your identity provider. You are then returned to the Portal.

The SSO settings page shows a **Sign-in link for your team** that skips step 2:
`https://cloud.ravendb.net/user/login/enterprise?domain=<your-account-domain>`.
Adding an SSO user sends no email, so share this link with your team. If you add RavenDB Cloud to your
identity provider's app dashboard, point the tile at this link — the sign-in must start at RavenDB Cloud.

* A person must already exist as an SSO user — added on the Users & Access page, or provisioned through
  SCIM. Signing in never creates users.
* The sign-in must be completed in the browser that started it, within 10 minutes.
* If your account restricts member email domains, an SSO user with an email address outside them is
  refused.

<Admonition type="note" title="The account owner">
The account owner never signs in through SSO and is never provisioned, deprovisioned or changed by
SCIM. The owner keeps using their own login, so a misconfigured connection cannot lock you out of the
account.
</Admonition>

<Admonition type="info" title="MFA">
Multi-factor authentication for SSO users is enforced by your identity provider. SSO users cannot turn
on RavenDB Cloud two-factor authentication; their Profile **Security** page shows
*Managed by your identity provider* instead. See [MFA](../cloud/cloud-security.mdx#mfa).
</Admonition>

**When SSO sign-in cannot complete**

| Message | What it means |
|---|---|
| You don't have access to this account yet. Contact your administrator. | No SSO user exists for you on the connection. Ask an administrator to add or provision you. |
| Your account does not have access to sign in through single sign-on. Contact your administrator. | You are not an active member of the account, your email domain is not allowed, or you are the account owner. |
| Single sign-on is not enabled for this account. | SSO is disabled, or the feature is not active for the account. |
| Your single sign-on session expired. Please try again. | The sign-in took longer than 10 minutes, or finished in a different browser. Start again. |
| Single sign-on failed. Please try again or contact your administrator. | The identity provider returned an error, the sign-in was cancelled, or the ID token failed validation. |
| Your identity provider reports that your email address is not verified, so it cannot be used to sign in. Ask your administrator to verify it. | The ID token marks the email as unverified. Verify it at the identity provider. |
| Single sign-on is misconfigured for this account. Please contact support. | The stored connection cannot be read. Contact [Support](../cloud/cloud-support.mdx). |

## Managing the connection

Open **Account features** and click **Manage** on the **External SSO & User Management** card.
The SSO settings page shows the **Redirect URL** and the **Management type**, and offers these actions:

* **Edit** — change the issuer, client ID or client secret. Leave the client secret empty to keep the
  stored one.
* **Change management type** — see [Management types](../cloud/cloud-sso.mdx#management-types).
* **Reset identity links** — clears the stored link between each SSO user and their identity at the
  provider. Each user is linked again at their next sign-in. Use it when users cannot sign in after the
  application was re-created at your identity provider.
* **Disable SSO** — stops sign-in through your identity provider.

<Admonition type="warning" title="Disabling SSO deletes the SSO setup">
Disabling SSO also **deletes every SSO user**, every discovered group, the group mapping and all SCIM
tokens. Enabling SSO again starts from an empty setup.
</Admonition>

<Admonition type="note" title="">
Changing the **issuer** or **client ID** resets the identity links automatically, because the identity
provider may issue new user identifiers for the new application.
</Admonition>

If the feature is deactivated for your account, SSO sign-in and SCIM requests stop, and the
configuration is kept.

## Troubleshooting

* **Sign-in fails although the identity provider shows a successful login** — check that the `email`
  claim is in the **ID token**, not only in the UserInfo response. This is the most common setup issue.
* **Sign-in fails right after the identity provider redirects back** — check that the provider accepts
  `client_secret_post` client authentication, and that the Redirect URL is registered exactly as shown.
* **SCIM requests return 404** — check the SCIM base URL: use it exactly as shown in the Portal, without
  adding `/scim/v2` or `/Users` to it.
* **SCIM requests are rejected as not configured** — the connection is not in
  *Sign-in and user provisioning (SCIM)* mode, or SSO is disabled.
* **The identity provider's connection test fails right after you create a token** — wait a few seconds
  and test again.
* **Everyone is locked out after the application was re-created at the identity provider** — use
  **Reset identity links**.
